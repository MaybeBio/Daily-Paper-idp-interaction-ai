## Reviewer 1
- **Overall assessment** This manuscript presents an ambitious theoretical framework that attempts to unify diverse biophysical concepts to explain epichaperome function. The scope is broad and the conceptual synthesis is creative. However, the current submission is essentially a narrative essay with mathematical terminology rather than a developed theory. The absence of any equations, quantitative predictions, or testable mathematical formulations makes it impossible to evaluate the internal consistency, predictive power, or novelty of the proposed framework. The manuscript reads as a research proposal or perspective piece, not as a primary research article establishing a new theory.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in chaperone biology, proteostasis, and stress biology might find the conceptual framework stimulating for generating new hypotheses. Biophysicists and systems biologists interested in active matter and cellular organization could be intrigued by the proposed integration of multiple theoretical approaches. However, without mathematical development, the audience is limited to those interested in qualitative conceptual models.
- **Major strengths** (1) The attempt to integrate multiple biophysical theories (electrostatics, transport, percolation, graph theory, condensate physics) into a unified framework for epichaperome function is intellectually ambitious and potentially valuable. (2) The extension to transcellular and intercellular scales (Transcellular Epichaperome Continuum Hypothesis) is a novel conceptual direction that could open new experimental avenues. (3) The authors explicitly acknowledge the speculative nature of the theory and identify testable predictions.
- **Major Concerns**
    - **Concern ID** R1-M1
    - **Severity** Major
    - **Blocking** Yes
    - **Axis** Theoretical development
    - **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims to develop a "mathematical description" of epichaperome function as adaptive organizational scaffolds.
    - **Evidence pointer** Results section (location not provided)
    - **Concern** The manuscript states that a mathematical description is developed by integrating principles from multiple theories, but no equations, mathematical derivations, or quantitative models are presented. The text only lists the names of theoretical frameworks (Poisson-Boltzmann, Nernst-Planck, percolation theory, etc.) without showing how they are combined, what assumptions are made, or what predictions emerge.
    - **Why it matters** A mathematical theory must contain explicit mathematical formulations. Without them, the framework is a qualitative conceptual model, not a mathematical theory. The claim of a "mathematical description" is unsupported.
    - **Resolution test** Provide the actual mathematical equations, define all variables and parameters, specify boundary conditions and assumptions, and show how the integration of different theories yields specific predictions.
    - **Concern ID** R1-M2
    - **Severity** Major
    - **Blocking** Yes
    - **Axis** Testability and predictions
    - **Claim pointer** The manuscript claims that "mathematical analysis predicts the emergence of percolating transport networks, electrostatic coupling domains, synchronized conformational dynamics, and stress-responsive communication pathways when epichaperome connectivity exceeds critical thresholds."
    - **Evidence pointer** Conclusions section (location not provided)
    - **Concern** No mathematical analysis is presented. The text asserts predictions without showing any calculations, simulations, or analytical results. Critical thresholds are mentioned but not defined or derived. The nature of the predicted phenomena (e.g., what constitutes a "percolating transport network" in molecular terms) is not specified.
    - **Why it matters** The core value of a theoretical framework is its ability to generate testable, quantitative predictions. Without any mathematical analysis, the predictions are merely verbal statements that cannot be evaluated or tested.
    - **Resolution test** Present the mathematical analysis that leads to these predictions. Define the critical thresholds, show how they are derived from the model, and specify quantitative predictions (e.g., "when epichaperome density exceeds X molecules/μm², percolation probability exceeds 0.5").
    - **Concern ID** R1-M3
    - **Severity** Major
    - **Blocking** Yes
    - **Axis** Novelty and prior art
    - **Claim pointer** The manuscript presents the Epichaperome Matrix Theory as a novel framework.
    - **Evidence pointer** Background and Results sections (location not provided)
    - **Concern** The manuscript does not discuss how the proposed framework differs from or extends existing theoretical models of chaperone networks, protein interaction networks, or biomolecular condensates. Concepts like electrostatic landscapes, percolation in protein networks, and active matter in cellular organization have been extensively developed in other contexts. The specific novelty of the epichaperome application is not established.

Explicitly state what new predictions or insights the epichaperome-specific framework provides that cannot be derived from existing theories.

    - **Scientific importance**: The topic (epichaperome function in stress biology and disease) is important, but the current manuscript does not establish that the proposed framework advances understanding beyond what is already known.
    - **Interdisciplinary readership**: The framework bridges chaperone biology, biophysics, and systems biology, which could attract broad interest if properly developed. Currently, the qualitative nature limits appeal to biophysicists.
    - **Technical soundness**: Cannot be assessed because no technical content (equations, simulations, data) is presented.

A major revision is required that includes explicit mathematical formulations, quantitative predictions, and a clear demonstration of novelty relative to existing models. The current submission is more appropriate for a perspective or review article than a primary research paper.
