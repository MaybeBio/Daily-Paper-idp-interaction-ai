## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript (including main text, figures, and supplementary materials)
- **Assessment boundary** The manuscript as submitted; no external data or prior reviews considered
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors propose a stepwise molecular pathway for the recruitment of the ULK1 autophagy-initiating complex to membranes, mediated by WIPI proteins and direct membrane interactions of the ATG101 WF finger, and show that a PVP motif in the ULK1 IDR binds the ATG13 HORMA domain to position the kinase domain near the membrane.
- **Visible evidence base** Biochemical reconstitution (GUV, SUV, liposome binding, kinase assays), cell-based assays (autophagy flux, mitophagy, co-IP, immunofluorescence), AlphaFold2 structural predictions, and atomistic molecular dynamics simulations.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** None identified; all key data appear to be presented.

## Reviewer 1
- **Overall assessment** This is an impressive and comprehensive study that addresses a long-standing question in the autophagy field: how the ULK1 complex is recruited to PI3P-containing membranes despite lacking a canonical PI3P-binding domain. The work is technically rigorous, combining in vitro reconstitution, cell biology, structural modeling, and MD simulations. The proposed stepwise model is compelling and well-supported by the data. The manuscript is clearly written and will be of broad interest.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Autophagy researchers, cell biologists studying membrane dynamics and kinase signaling, and structural biologists interested in HORMA domain proteins and membrane-protein interactions. The work provides a mechanistic framework for understanding a central event in autophagy initiation and has implications for diseases linked to autophagy dysfunction, such as Parkinson’s disease.
- **Major strengths** 1. Addresses a fundamental, unresolved question in autophagy with a clear and testable model. 2. Employs a powerful combination of in vitro reconstitution, cell-based assays, and computational modeling. 3. The evidence is internally consistent and mutually reinforcing across different experimental systems. 4. The identification of the ULK1 PVP motif as a direct HORMA-domain binder is a novel and important finding.
- **Major Concerns**
    - **Concern ID** R1-M1
    - **Severity** Major
    - **Blocking** No
    - **Axis** Interpretation / Overclaim
    - **Claim pointer** The authors state that the data "establish a stepwise pathway for recruitment of the ULK1 KD to the vicinity of the membrane surface" (Abstract) and that the model "accounts for" the PI3P-dependent stabilization of ULK1C (Discussion).
    - **Evidence pointer** Abstract, Discussion (paragraph 1)
    - **Concern** While the data strongly support a role for WIPI3 and the ATG101 WF finger in ULK1C recruitment, the claim that this is the *sole* or *primary* mechanism explaining PI3P-dependent stabilization is not fully established. The authors acknowledge that WIPI2 also binds ATG13 (via a different site) and that other mechanisms (e.g., supercomplex formation with PI3KC3-C1, ATG8 binding) likely contribute. The relative contribution of the WIPI3 pathway versus these other pathways is not addressed. The model is presented as a definitive "stepwise pathway," but the data are more consistent with a parallel, redundant, or synergistic set of interactions.
    - **Why it matters** Overstating the exclusivity of the proposed pathway could mislead the field. The title and abstract should more accurately reflect that this is a *key* or *novel* mechanism, not necessarily the *complete* or *sole* pathway.
    - **Resolution test** The authors should temper the language in the Abstract and Discussion to reflect that this is a newly identified, important mechanism that operates alongside other known recruitment pathways. A more nuanced model figure (Fig. 7) showing parallel inputs would be helpful.
    - **Concern ID** R1-M2
    - **Severity** Major
    - **Blocking** No
    - **Axis** Technical Rigor / Missing Control
    - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that the ATG13:ATG101 HORMA dimer does not bind PI3P, based on GUV binding assays (Introduction, Results).
    - **Evidence pointer** Introduction (paragraph 4), Results (paragraph 1 of "ATG101 binds directly...")
    - **Concern** The authors state that they "have not been able to replicate the finding of PI3P binding using more stringent and biologically realistic GUV binding assays." However, the data for this negative result are not shown in the main figures or supplementary materials. The only GUV data shown (Fig. 2) are in the presence of WIPI3. A control experiment showing that ATG13:ATG101 alone does not bind to PI3P-containing GUVs is essential to support this key claim.
    - **Why it matters** The entire premise of the paper rests on the idea that ULK1C lacks intrinsic PI3P-binding capacity. If this negative result is not rigorously demonstrated, the rationale for the study is weakened.
    - **Resolution test** The authors should include a control experiment (e.g., in Fig. 2 or a supplementary figure) showing that ATG13:ATG101 (WT and mutants) does not bind to PI3P-containing GUVs in the absence of WIPI proteins, across a range of protein concentrations.
    - **Concern ID** R1-M3
    - **Severity** Major
    - **Blocking** No
    - **Axis** Interpretation / Overclaim
    - **Claim pointer** The authors claim that the ULK1 PVP motif "docks onto the ATG13:ATG101 HORMA dimer" and that this interaction "brings the ULK1 KD close to the membrane" (Abstract, Results).
    - **Evidence pointer** Abstract, Results (Fig. 5)
    - **Concern** The evidence for the functional consequence of the PVP-ATG13 interaction is strong (loss of phosphorylation, autophagy, mitophagy). However, the direct evidence that this interaction *physically brings the KD closer to the membrane* is based entirely on computational modeling (Fig. 5H-J). While the modeling is well-executed, it is a prediction, not a direct measurement. The claim in the Abstract and Results should be more carefully qualified.
    - **Why it matters** The central mechanistic claim of the paper is that the PVP motif positions the KD. If this is only supported by modeling, it should be stated as such. Overstating the evidence could be misleading.
    - **Resolution test** The authors should add a qualifier in the Abstract and Results (e.g., "computational modeling suggests that...") or, ideally, provide experimental evidence (e.g., FRET, proximity labeling) to support the distance change.
- **Minor Comments**
    - **Concern ID** R1-m1
    - **Severity** Minor
    - **Axis** Clarity
    - **Affected element** Figure 1
    - **Evidence pointer** Fig. 1A, B
    - **Issue** The sequence logo in Fig. 1A is described as showing the "DHF motif," but the logo appears to show conservation of a broader region. The exact boundaries of the motif should be more clearly indicated.
    - **Required correction** Add a box or bracket to the sequence logo to explicitly define the DHF motif (residues 213-215).
    - **Concern ID** R1-m2
    - **Severity** Minor
    - **Axis** Clarity
    - **Affected element** Figure 2
    - **Evidence pointer** Fig. 2D, E
    - **Issue** The GUV binding data in Fig. 2D, E show a "linear, concentration-dependent relationship" for the WF mutant. The text states this is "primarily driven by the WIPI3-PI3P interaction." This is a reasonable interpretation, but it is not directly tested (e.g., with a WIPI3 mutant that cannot bind PI3P).
    - **Required correction** The authors should either add a control experiment (e.g., WIPI3 LRRG mutant) or soften the language to "consistent with being primarily driven by..."
    - **Concern ID** R1-m3
    - **Severity** Minor
    - **Axis** Presentation
    - **Affected element** Figure 4
    - **Evidence pointer** Fig. 4B, C
    - **Issue** The dot blot data in Fig. 4B are semi-quantitative. The quantification in Fig. 4C is helpful, but the error bars appear to be from technical replicates. It is not clear if the experiments were performed with biological replicates.
    - **Required correction** Clarify in the figure legend whether the data represent biological or technical replicates. If technical, a statement about reproducibility across independent experiments should be added.
    - **Concern ID** R1-m4
    - **Severity** Minor
    - **Axis** Clarity
    - **Affected element** Methods
    - **Evidence pointer** "Membrane reconstitution assay"
    - **Issue** The reconstitution assay uses a "minimal substrate" of ATG16L1 (78-300). It is not clear if this fragment is membrane-associated in the assay, or if it is soluble. The text implies it is a membrane-associated substrate, but the methods do not describe how it is tethered to the liposomes.
    - **Required correction** Clarify whether the ATG16L1 fragment is directly bound to the liposomes (e.g., via a lipid anchor or a WIPI interaction) or is soluble in the assay.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M2 (missing negative control for PI3P binding) is the most critical technical issue. R1-M1 and R1-M3 are more about interpretation and should be addressed to strengthen the paper.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria**
    - **Originality** High. The study provides a novel and unexpected mechanism for ULK1 recruitment, solving a long-standing puzzle.
    - **Scientific importance** High. The work addresses a central, unresolved question in autophagy and has implications for understanding disease mechanisms.
    - **Interdisciplinary readership** High. The work will be of interest to cell biologists, biochemists, structural biologists, and those studying membrane dynamics and kinase signaling.
    - **Technical soundness** High. The study uses a rigorous, multi-pronged approach. The main technical concern is the missing negative control for PI3P binding.
    - **Readability for nonspecialists** Good. The paper is well-structured and clearly written, with a helpful model figure. Some of the mechanistic details may be challenging for a complete outsider, but the main narrative is accessible.

## Recommendation posture
Supportive if the major concerns are addressed. The missing negative control (R1-M2) is essential. The over-interpretation issues (R1-M1, R1-M3) should be corrected to ensure the claims are accurately represented. The paper is otherwise excellent and makes a significant contribution.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim that the ATG13:ATG101 HORMA dimer does not bind PI3P is not supported by the data shown in the manuscript. A negative control experiment is required.
- The claim that the PVP motif interaction "brings the ULK1 KD close to the membrane" is supported only by computational modeling and should be presented as a prediction, not a demonstrated fact.
