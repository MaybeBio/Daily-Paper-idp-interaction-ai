## Review setup
- **Input scope** Full manuscript (abstract only provided)
- **Assessment boundary** Abstract content only
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report that low-molecular-weight Guangdong Choy Sum polysaccharides (GCSP, Mw = 71.49 kDa) can be used to construct cold-set composite hydrogels with gelatin, which exhibit improved rheological properties for dysphagia diets and enhanced anthocyanin photostability. The mechanism is attributed to hydrogen bonding and electrostatic interactions leading to microstructural densification.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract only; no figures, tables, methods, or results sections provided
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript (Methods, Results, Discussion, Figures, Tables, Supplementary Information)

## Reviewer 1
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents an interesting concept of using a low-molecular-weight plant polysaccharide to modulate gelatin hydrogel properties for dual food applications. However, the provided evidence is insufficient to evaluate the validity, novelty, or technical soundness of the claims. A full manuscript is required for proper assessment.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in food hydrocolloids, dysphagia food design, and anthocyanin stabilization would be interested. The concept of using a low-Mw polysaccharide to avoid phase separation in protein-polysaccharide systems is potentially relevant to the broader field of texture-modified foods.
- **Major strengths** 1. The dual-functionality approach (dysphagia diet + anthocyanin stabilization) is a practical and potentially impactful application. 2. The use of a low-Mw polysaccharide to address the known problem of phase separation in high-Mw polysaccharide-gelatin systems is a logical and potentially novel strategy.
- **Major Concerns**
    - **Concern ID** R1-M1
    - **Severity** Major
    - **Blocking** Yes
    - **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
    - **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that GCSP forms strong hydrogen bonding and electrostatic interactions with gelatin, leading to microstructural densification and reduced porosity.
    - **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; "DLS and FTIR characterizations corroborated... SEM observations validated..."
    - **Concern** The abstract provides only qualitative statements about characterization results (DLS, FTIR, SEM) without any quantitative data, statistical analysis, or experimental details. The reported porosity values (78.5% to 36.2%) are presented without error bars, sample size, or imaging conditions.
    - **Why it matters** Without access to the actual spectra, micrographs, and data analysis, it is impossible to verify the claimed interactions, the extent of densification, or whether the observed effects are statistically significant and reproducible.
    - **Resolution test** Provide the full manuscript with all figures, tables, and detailed methods. The DLS data should show size distributions, FTIR should show peak shifts, and SEM should show representative images with scale bars and quantitative porosity analysis.

    - **Concern ID** R1-M2
    - **Severity** Major
    - **Blocking** Yes
    - **Axis** Claim support
    - **Claim pointer** The abstract claims that the composite hydrogels comply with IDDSI Level 4/5 standards and are suitable for safe dysphagia food.
    - **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; "Swallowing simulations complied with IDDSI Level 4/5 standards."
    - **Concern** The abstract does not specify which IDDSI tests were performed (e.g., flow test, fork drip test, spoon tilt test), the exact rheological parameters measured, or how the results map to the IDDSI framework. The claim of "safe" dysphagia food is a strong statement that requires evidence of appropriate texture, cohesiveness, and absence of aspiration risk.
    - **Why it matters** IDDSI compliance is a critical claim for the application. Without detailed methods and results, the claim is unverifiable. The safety implication requires rigorous evidence.
    - **Resolution test** Provide the full methods and results for IDDSI testing, including specific test protocols, numerical thresholds, and comparison to IDDSI guidelines.

    - **Concern ID** R1-M3
    - **Severity** Major
    - **Blocking** Yes
    - **Axis** Novelty and significance
    - **Claim pointer** The abstract implies that the work is novel in elucidating "polysaccharide-regulated protein rearrangement" and offering a "dual-functional matrix."
    - **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; no comparison to prior work.
    - **Concern** The abstract does not cite or discuss any prior work on low-Mw polysaccharide-gelatin hydrogels, polysaccharide-regulated protein rearrangement, or dual-functional dysphagia/anthocyanin systems. Without this context, the novelty and significance of the work cannot be assessed.
    - **Why it matters** The claim of novelty is a central criterion for publication. The abstract must demonstrate how this work advances beyond existing literature.
    - **Resolution test** Provide a full introduction and discussion that clearly situates the work within the existing literature and articulates the specific advance.

- **Minor Comments**
    - **Concern ID** R1-m1
    - **Severity** Minor
    - **Axis** Clarity
    - **Affected element** Abstract text
    - **Evidence pointer** Abstract
    - **Issue** The phrase "mitigating brittleness and excessive stickiness" is vague. It is unclear which specific rheological parameters (e.g., storage modulus, loss tangent, adhesiveness) are being referred to.
    - **Required correction** Specify the rheological parameters that were measured and how they relate to brittleness and stickiness.

    - **Concern ID** R1-m2
    - **Severity** Minor
    - **Axis** Completeness
    - **Affected element** Abstract text
    - **Evidence pointer** Abstract
    - **Issue** The anthocyanin photostability data is presented as "retaining over 40% after 10-day UV exposure" without specifying the initial concentration, the type of anthocyanin, or the UV exposure conditions (intensity, wavelength).
    - **Required correction** Provide these details in the abstract or indicate that they are in the full manuscript.

- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** R1-M1, R1-M2, R1-M3. The core claims of mechanism, application compliance, and novelty are all unverifiable from the abstract alone.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** 
    - **Originality**: Cannot be assessed. The concept is plausible but the abstract does not demonstrate how it differs from existing work on polysaccharide-protein hydrogels.
    - **Scientific importance**: Potentially moderate. The dual-function approach is practical, but the fundamental insight into "polysaccharide-regulated protein rearrangement" needs to be demonstrated as a generalizable principle, not just a specific case.
    - **Interdisciplinary readership**: Moderate. The work bridges food science, materials science, and nutrition, but the abstract is too narrow to attract a broad audience.
    - **Technical soundness**: Not assessable from the abstract. The reported data lacks statistical rigor and experimental details.
    - **Readability for nonspecialists**: Acceptable. The abstract is clearly written and the main concepts are understandable.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. A full manuscript is required for a meaningful evaluation. The abstract alone does not provide sufficient evidence to support the claims of mechanism, IDDSI compliance, or novelty.

## Risk / unsupported claims
- The claim of "strong hydrogen bonding and electrostatic interactions" is unsupported without FTIR and DLS data.
- The claim of "microstructural densification" and specific porosity values is unsupported without SEM images and quantitative analysis.
- The claim of "compliance with IDDSI Level 4/5 standards" is unsupported without specific test results.
- The claim of "dual-functional matrix for safe elderly dysphagia food" is unsupported without evidence of safety and efficacy.
- The claim of "elucidating polysaccharide-regulated protein rearrangement" is unsupported without mechanistic studies and comparison to prior models.
