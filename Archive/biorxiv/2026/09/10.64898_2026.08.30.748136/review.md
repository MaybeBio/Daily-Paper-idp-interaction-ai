## Reviewer 1
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents an intriguing and potentially impactful framework for proteome-scale binding-site retrieval. However, the provided material is too limited to assess the validity, reproducibility, or significance of the claims. Several critical methodological and evaluative details are missing.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in computational structural biology, drug discovery, and proteomics would be interested. The ability to predict off-target binding sites at proteome scale without training could accelerate drug safety assessment and target identification.
- **Major strengths** The training-free approach using frozen PLM embeddings is conceptually elegant and avoids the need for task-specific fine-tuning. The reported top ranking on the ProSPECCTs benchmark (1st of 23 methods) is a strong claim if substantiated.
- **Major Concerns**
    - **Concern ID** R1-M1
    - **Severity** Major
    - **Blocking** Yes
    - **Axis** Methodological clarity and reproducibility
    - **Claim pointer** "PocketScope, a training-free framework that represents cavity-lining residues using frozen ESM-C 600M embeddings and retrieves related binding sites through exhaustive late-interaction MaxSim"
    - **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; no methods section provided
    - **Concern** The abstract does not specify how "cavity-lining residues" are defined or extracted from the AlphaFold structures. The method for identifying 153,805 cavities across 37,682 proteins is not described. The "late-interaction MaxSim" mechanism is mentioned but not explained.
    - **Why it matters** Without this information, the method cannot be reproduced or evaluated. The core technical innovation is opaque.
    - **Resolution test** The full manuscript must provide a clear, step-by-step description of cavity detection, residue selection, embedding extraction, and the MaxSim retrieval procedure.
    - **Concern ID** R1-M2
    - **Severity** Major
    - **Blocking** Yes
    - **Axis** Benchmarking and validation
    - **Claim pointer** "On the ProSPECCTs benchmark, PocketScope ranks 1st of 23 methods by mean rank across the ten collections."
    - **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; no benchmark details provided
    - **Concern** The abstract reports a single aggregate metric (mean rank across ten collections) without any breakdown, statistical significance, or comparison to baselines. The ProSPECCTs benchmark is not described, and it is unclear whether the comparison is fair (e.g., same input structures, same cavity definitions).
    - **Why it matters** A top ranking is a strong claim that requires rigorous, transparent benchmarking. Without details, the claim is unverifiable.
    - **Resolution test** The full manuscript must provide per-collection results, statistical tests (e.g., confidence intervals), a description of the benchmark, and a clear statement of how other methods were run or cited.
    - **Concern ID** R1-M3
    - **Severity** Major
    - **Blocking** Yes
    - **Axis** Validation of biological relevance
    - **Claim pointer** "PocketScope identified 153,805 cavities across 37,682 proteins in the AlphaFold human proteome and recovered documented drug off-targets across a curated set of pharmacological pairs."
    - **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; no results provided
    - **Concern** The abstract claims recovery of documented off-targets but provides no quantitative measure (e.g., recall, precision, enrichment factor). The "curated set of pharmacological pairs" is not described.
    - **Why it matters** The central biological claim of the work—that PocketScope enables off-target prediction—is unsupported by any evidence in the abstract.
    - **Resolution test** The full manuscript must present quantitative retrieval performance on the off-target set, including appropriate baselines and statistical evaluation.
